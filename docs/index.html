01 — The Attention Hierarchy

Three tiers of light.
Zero interruptions.

Periphery maps the urgency of every event to a visual cue drawn on a transparent, click-through canvas over your screen. Nothing steals focus. Nothing makes a sound. Press a trigger below and watch it happen — right here, on this page.

Tier 1 · Break flow

The Comet

A single bright streak across your screen with a fast pulse. Reserved for the rare things that genuinely outrank your flow.

Direct manager DMs · Sev-1 incidents

Tier 2 · Awareness

The Edge Glow

A subtle breath of light along the edge of the display. Enough to register a context shift, never enough to break a thought.

CI/CD pipeline failures · Upcoming meetings

Tier 3 · Ambient

The Bottom Glow

A slow warmth rising from the bottom of the screen, like a horizon. Background state you absorb without ever looking at it.

Pomodoro breaks · CC’d email

Special class · Coding agents

The Agent Beacon

When Claude Code, Devin, or Copilot finishes a long task, a violet glow breathes in the corner — and doesn’t expire. It waits until you’re demonstrably back at the keyboard, replays its message once, and fades. A subtle glow can be missed. The beacon cannot.

Special class · Waiting on you

The Blocked Beacon

An agent stalled on your approval burns wall-clock time every second, so this is the one cue that gets more insistent on its own — brighter, larger, faster with age, bounded at three levels. Never a comet, never a sound. And being back at the keyboard doesn’t clear it: presence isn’t approval.

02 — Attention-aware delivery

It doesn’t just look quiet.
It waits for you.

Beyond how a cue looks, Periphery decides when it deserves your eyes. Every cue passes through the same gauntlet: focus hold → slack tide → immediate. Only Tier 1 cues and meeting reminders go straight through.

Slack Tide

While you’re mid-keystroke-burst, non-urgent cues are held and released in your next natural micro-pause — about six seconds without input. Pills never pile up, nothing is held longer than 90 seconds, and a flood collapses into a single +N more. No keylogging: just the OS idle timer.

you, mid-flow

 

The Constellation

Cues held during focus don’t vanish into a counter. Each leaves a dim, twinkling star near the corner of your screen. A glance tells you how much happened — without telling you what, which is exactly the information that breaks flow. When focus ends, the stars dissolve into one quiet summary.

5 updates — gitlab 3 · outlook 2

Focus Assist, actually respected

While Windows would suppress toasts — Focus Assist, presentation mode, a full-screen app — Periphery holds everything but Tier 1 and meeting reminders, then flushes one quiet summary glow when focus ends. Your DND setting finally means what it says.

03 — Privacy & security

Private by architecture.
Not by policy.

Periphery was built for strict enterprise environments where data exfiltration and cloud dependencies are unacceptable. There is no privacy setting to trust — there is simply nowhere for your data to go.

No cloud backend

No central server. No telemetry. The only outbound requests are the ones you configure — straight to GitLab and Microsoft Graph, from your machine. The overlay never loads a single byte from the network.

Encrypted secret storage

Tokens are encrypted with your OS credential store and only ciphertext touches disk. No preload bridge can read a secret back out.

DPAPI · Keychain · libsecret — bound to your user account

Loopback-only webhook

The local cue receiver binds to loopback and is unreachable from the network, with allowlist validation on every untrusted payload.

bind 127.0.0.1:49123 · cuePayload allowlist

No third-party brokers

Integrations run locally, so there’s no Zapier, no Make.com, no automation middleman holding your credentials on the wrong side of the corporate firewall.

Renderers run sandboxed · context isolation · restrictive CSP · narrow contextBridge APIs

04 — The Connector Engine

Wired to your tools.
Not to their clouds.

A modular ConnectorManager polls APIs directly from your machine. Connectors receive credentials through an injected secret store — they never import Electron, which keeps them unit-tested, portable, and honest.

Your Teams presence can hold the line too: in a call, presenting, or on Do Not Disturb, ambient cues wait — read straight from Microsoft Graph, opt-in.

And for everything else, a lightweight local webhook lets your existing tools speak light — native hooks in Git, npm, or Docker can fire a cue with one request. No plugin required, ever.

05 — Zero friction

Calm shouldn’t be
a chore.

First cue in 60 seconds

A first-run wizard detects your git repos, package.json, and Docker — then writes the webhook recipes for you: a post-commit hook, notify:* scripts, a copyable Docker snippet. It never overwrites anything you wrote.

Installs like an app

One-click installer, start-at-login, and delta auto-updates that download quietly, announce themselves as a single ambient glow, and install when you quit. Never a popup — even the updater speaks light.

Honest about failure

An expired token or a rate-limited API becomes an amber dot on the tray icon with the reason in the tooltip, and a live banner in Settings — not a silent log line while your cues quietly stop arriving.

every behaviour above is a settings toggle · nothing is forced

Tuned first for Windows 11. Degrading gracefully everywhere else.

Accent-colour harmony

Cues without an explicit colour inherit your Windows accent — live, the moment you change it.

Fluent settings

Mica backdrop, Segoe UI Variable, Win11 cards and toggles that follow the system theme.

Tray as status

The tooltip says what Periphery is doing; held cues add a small accent badge dot. That’s the whole UI.

Power & motion aware

Cheaper animations on battery; with reduce-motion set, travelling cues become stationary fades.
